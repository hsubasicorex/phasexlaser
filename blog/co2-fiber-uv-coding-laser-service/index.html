Coding and marking

CO2, fiber and UV laser coders: is it a marking problem or an inspection problem?

A code that a person can read and a code the vision system accepts are two different observations. Separating them, and naming the laser family and the substrate, is what makes a coder service request useful.

"The codes are failing." It is a sentence that hides a fork in the road. A code an operator can read perfectly well may still be rejected by the vision system. A code the vision system passes may be unreadable at the far end of the supply chain. Which of those is happening decides whether this is a laser call at all.

Two different observations, routinely reported as one

Separate them before anything else, because they send the investigation in different directions.

What you seeWhat it tends to point at
The mark itself is faint, thin, broken or shallow to the eye The marking side: laser, optics, beam delivery, focus and standoff, or a material that is no longer responding the same way.
The mark looks fine but the inspection system rejects it Contrast, grade or position against what the reader expects: substrate colour and finish, mark placement, lighting and the reader’s own setup and tolerances.
The mark is in the wrong place, or drifts along the web or the case Timing and line integration: triggers, encoders, product detection, line speed and the controller’s job setup.
It marks at low line speed and fails at production speed Something that only appears under load. Worth reporting exactly that way rather than as an intermittent fault.

If both marking and inspection equipment are involved, give us the model of each. A request that names only the coder cannot describe an inspection complaint, and the two are often on the same line under the same job number.

The laser family is context, not a verdict

CO2, fiber and ultraviolet lasers all appear on packaging lines and they are not interchangeable. Domino’s own lineup is a useful illustration of the range rather than a ranking: its Dx-Series CO2 markers are presented for flexible films and for translucent materials such as glass, its F-Series fiber machines for metals and harder surfaces, and its U-Series UV markers for plastics, where UV is set apart by producing contrast on both light and dark plastic substrates on the same line.

Two things follow from that. The first is that "fiber is for metal" is too narrow to describe what is installed in the field: Domino states plainly that fiber lasers are also capable of marking plastics, and plenty of lines run them that way. The second is that the material decides more than the laser name does. Domino puts it directly: materials absorb and react to laser energy very differently, and it is the material characteristics that dictate which laser is compatible, which is why a laser project starts in a sample lab with the actual substrate.

Which is why a packaging change is relevant

Two cartons or two films can look identical and carry different coatings, pigments or layer structures. A new packaging stock, a new supplier for the same stock, or a different batch can therefore sit behind a code-quality complaint while the product and the artwork look unchanged.

That is not the same as saying a material change explains every problem. It means the material belongs in the request next to the machine, so that nobody spends a day on the laser before anyone thinks to ask what it is marking. If you have a sample of the old stock and the new one, keep them.

Say what it is doing to production

Faint, incomplete, displaced, inconsistent between products, or absent altogether: those are five different descriptions and each one is useful. Add any error message in the equipment’s own words, and say whether the line is stopped, running with a limitation, or available for planned work. A photograph of the code helps and will not on its own identify a failed source, a control problem or a mismatch elsewhere on the line.

Coders are part of a line, and we service the line

We work on packaging laser coders together with the control, scanning, electrical, extraction and line-interface equipment around them, and on the other coding and marking systems listed on our service pages, including the non-laser equipment that runs beside them. A coder is rarely a problem in isolation, and treating the laser source name as a description of the whole machine is how a line stays down longer than it needs to.

Send the manufacturer, model and serial number, the substrate, what the code looks like now, and whether the complaint is about the mark or about the reader. We reply with an assessment and, where bench or site work makes sense, a written scope and a fixed fee.
