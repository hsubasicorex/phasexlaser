Understand the machine

What is on the line

Laser coders mark date, lot, batch and serialisation data directly onto packaging as it moves. There is no consumable ink, so the running cost is low and the failure modes are different: they are optical, thermal and electronic rather than fluid.

The three source types on packaging lines each mark a different substrate. Sealed CO2 is the workhorse for board, paper, glass and many films. Fiber marks metals and some plastics. UV marks heat-sensitive materials and gives high contrast on films where CO2 would damage the substrate. Knowing which one is in the cabinet is the first step in any diagnosis.

Why they fail

What actually goes wrong

01

Output window and lens contamination

Product dust, paper fibre, condensation and vapour on the exit optics. The most common cause of a coder that has quietly lost contrast, and the cheapest to fix once confirmed.

02

Extraction loss

Fume extraction that has lost flow puts debris back on the optics and changes the mark. Presents as an optics fault, is really an airflow fault.

03

Focus and standoff drift

After a line change, a guide adjustment or a knock, the marking distance is no longer what the job expects. Contrast drops without any component having failed.

04

Galvo scanner faults

Scanner drift, field distortion and positional error. Shows as distorted characters, drifting position or codes that fail verification.

05

Source decay

Genuine output loss over the source's life, measured against the machine reference rather than inferred from the mark.

06

Drive and supply electronics

RF drive on CO2 sources, pump drive on fiber, and the supplies behind both, including age-related capacitor failure in long-serving units.

07

Cooling

Air or liquid cooling paths, filters and fans. Thermal derating shows as a coder that works at the start of a shift and fades through it.

08

Control, interface and interlock

Line interfaces, encoder and trigger inputs, interlocks and the control PC or embedded software on units well past their original support.

Why the legacy list matters

Three of the largest coder fleets on packaging lines are models their manufacturers have moved to legacy status. Domino lists the D120i, D320i, D520i and D620i on its own legacy pages, superseded by the Dx-Series. Videojet's 3120 and 3320 were superseded by the 3140, 3340 and 3640. Markem-Imaje's 7031 family, including the 9.3 and 10.2 micron wavelength variants, predates the SmartLase generation. Panasonic publishes its own discontinued list covering the LP-S, LP-Z, LP-M, LP-GS, LP-V, LP-W, LP-200, LP-300, LP-400 and LP-F. None of that stops the machines working, and all of it changes how spares are planned.

One thing to check on Koenig & Bauer coders

KBA-Metronic was renamed Koenig & Bauer Coding in July 2018, but the old kba-metronic.com site is still live and still shows pre-2018 laser pages. If you are working from documentation found there, check it against the current Koenig & Bauer Coding material before ordering anything.

Scope of work

What we repair and what we replace on a coding laser

Repair on this equipment is not one job. It is a set of different jobs, and which one you need depends on what the evaluation finds. The table below is the honest version of what that work looks like: what gets opened and put right, and what gets taken out and replaced. Which column a given fault lands in is decided by measurement, not by preference.

Two rules run through all of it. Anything we replace is documented with what came out and what went in, and anything we repair is validated by measurement afterwards rather than by whether the machine switches on. If a part turns out to be a failed custom assembly with no substitute available, we say so in writing instead of billing an attempt.

Obsolete and unavailable parts

A discontinued part is a sourcing and engineering problem, not automatically the end of the machine. Depending on the item, the route is a current-production equivalent fitted and verified, a qualified substitute with the differences written down, a harness or cable made to suit, or a board repaired at component level because the board itself is no longer sold. We tell you which of those we are proposing before you approve it.

Process

How a job runs

  1. Enquiry. Coder model and serial number, substrate, what the mark looks like now, and when it changed.
  2. Written scope. A fixed evaluation fee and the measurements to be taken, quoted before anyone travels.
  3. Evaluation. Delivered power measured against the machine reference, beam path and optics inspected, extraction and cooling checked, scanner and interface behaviour verified.
  4. Written findings and your approval. The numbers, the fault, the options and the cost. Nothing is repaired without written approval.
  5. Repair, validation and report. Measured validation against the reference and a written service report for your maintenance records.

Scope and safety

Two limits

  • We do not promise a specific repair before diagnosis.
  • We do not certify a machine to original published specifications where the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards are unavailable.

Laser safety on a production line

Most packaging coders run as Class 1 enclosed systems in normal production. Service work opens that enclosure. It is planned and executed under a project-specific laser safety plan agreed with your plant, with wavelength-rated eye protection, a defined and terminated beam path and controlled access. Final hazard analysis and approval stay with your own EHS function.

Questions plants ask

Our coder is a discontinued model. Is it worth repairing?

Frequently yes. Coders are built from a sealed source, a galvo scanner, a controller and a supply, and those are serviceable at component level. Several of the largest fleets on packaging lines are models the manufacturer has moved to a legacy page, which means spares planning matters but does not mean the machine is finished.

The code is fading or inconsistent. Is the laser failing?

Not necessarily. Faded marking is as often a contaminated output window, a lens that has collected product dust, extraction that has lost flow, or a focus that has drifted after a line change. Measuring delivered power and inspecting the beam path separates those from genuine source decay.

Can you work on the laser without stopping the whole line?

Usually the coder can be isolated, evaluated and worked on while the rest of the line is down for a planned window. We agree the access window and the safety arrangements with your plant before we arrive.

Do you reprogram the coder or manage the message content?

We handle the laser, optical, thermal and control hardware and the interfaces to your line. Message content, product data and code verification stay with your own systems and quality function.

Next step

Codes fading or failing verification?

Send the coder model and serial number, the substrate, and a photograph of the mark as it looks now. We respond with the next step and a fixed evaluation fee before anyone travels.
