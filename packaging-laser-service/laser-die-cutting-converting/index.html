Understand the machine

What these machines are

Laser converting equipment cuts, creases, scores, perforates and kiss-cuts board, label stock and flexible film without a steel rule die. The cutting is done by a laser beam steered across the material, so the tool never wears and a job change is a file change.

For the service engineer the important point is that the machine builder rarely built the laser. These systems are assembled around a bought-in sealed CO2 or fiber source, beam delivery, galvo or gantry motion, extraction, cooling, drive electronics and a control system. Each of those is serviceable on its own terms.

Why they fail

Where converting lasers lose performance

01

Source output decay

Cut quality falls off gradually: edges char, the machine needs more passes, or thicker stock stops cutting through. Established on the equipment rather than judged by eye.

02

Beam delivery contamination

Mirrors, lenses, output windows and protective optics collect debris from the material being cut. This is the single most common cause of a converting laser that has quietly lost power.

03

Extraction and air assist

Fume and particulate extraction that has lost flow puts debris back onto the optics and changes the cut. It presents as an optics problem and it is really an airflow problem.

04

Cooling

Chillers, flow rates, coolant condition and temperature control. A thermal problem shows as drifting power and inconsistent cuts across a long run rather than as an alarm.

05

Galvo, scanner and motion

Scanner drift, bearing wear, encoder faults and registration error. Shows as dimensional drift, corner quality loss or cuts that do not close.

06

RF or driver electronics

On RF-excited CO2 sources, the drive electronics and their supplies. On fiber sources, the pump drive and its protection behaviour.

07

Control and software path

Control PCs, motion controllers and job software that are years past their operating system support, on machines that still run production every day.

Scope of work

What we repair and what we replace on a converting laser

Repair on this equipment is not one job. It is a set of different jobs, and which one you need depends on what the evaluation finds. The table below is the honest version of what that work looks like: what gets opened and put right, and what gets taken out and replaced. Which column a given fault lands in is decided by what the evaluation finds, and it is stated in writing with a price before anything is agreed.

Two rules run through all of it. Anything we replace is documented with what came out and what went in, and anything we repair is validated by measurement afterwards rather than by whether the machine switches on. If a part turns out to be a failed custom assembly with no substitute available, we say so in writing instead of billing an attempt.

Obsolete and unavailable parts

A discontinued part is a sourcing and engineering problem, not automatically the end of the machine. Depending on the item, the route is a current-production equivalent fitted and verified, a qualified substitute with the differences written down, a harness or cable made to suit, or a board repaired at component level because the board itself is no longer sold. We tell you which of those we are proposing before you approve it.

Process

How a job runs

  1. Enquiry. Machine, model, serial number, what changed and any measurements you already have. We tell you whether a site visit or a bench scope is the right shape.
  2. Written scope and fixed evaluation fee. Quoted before anyone travels, stating what will be examined, what instrumentation will be used, and what a negative result would mean.
  3. On-site or bench evaluation. The as-found condition of the machine recorded, and every subsystem covered on its own terms: source and beam delivery, optics, extraction, cooling, motion and controls.
  4. Written findings. What was measured, what was found, what remains unknown, and the repair options with their cost and risks.
  5. Your approval. Nothing is repaired, substituted or modified until you approve the scope and price in writing.
  6. Repair, validation and report. Work inside the approved scope, then validation that the agreed scope was met, and a written service report.

Scope and safety

Two limits

  • We do not promise a specific repair before diagnosis. The evaluation comes first and the repair scope follows from it.
  • We do not certify a machine to original published specifications where the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards are unavailable. We document the measured condition before and after.

Laser safety

Class 4 work is planned and executed under a project-specific laser safety plan. The required engineering controls, personal protective equipment, beam management and facility conditions are defined in writing before any energized optical work begins. On a customer site, final hazard analysis and approval stay with the plant's own safety function.

Questions converters ask

Our laser cutter's manufacturer is gone. Can it still be supported?

In most cases yes. These machines are built from identifiable subsystems: a CO2 or fiber source, beam delivery and galvo or gantry motion, extraction, chillers, drive electronics and a control PC. Those are serviceable at component level whether or not the original builder still exists. What ends a repair is a failed custom assembly with no available substitute, and we establish that before quoting.

Who actually made the laser inside my converting machine?

Often not the machine builder: packaging converting machines are commonly built around a bought-in sealed CO2 or fiber source, and the source market has consolidated into a small number of houses. Identifying the source and its condition separately from the machine is the first step in any sensible diagnosis.

Can you work on the laser without touching the converting line?

Yes, and that is often the right scope. The laser, beam delivery and extraction can be evaluated and repaired as a subsystem while the web handling, registration and finishing sections stay as they are. We state the boundary in writing before work starts.

Do you supply replacement dies or tooling?

No. We work on the laser, optical, thermal, motion and control subsystems. Tooling, dies and consumables come from your existing supply chain, and we will tell you plainly when a problem is a tooling problem rather than a machine problem.
