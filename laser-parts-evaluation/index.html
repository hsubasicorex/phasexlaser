What this is

Someone holding a failed board, a dead driver or a part with a number nobody recognises has a specific problem, and it is not usually where to buy it. It is what this actually is, whether it is even the part that failed, and what to do if it is no longer made. That has an engineering answer, and giving it is what we do.

What to send

All of it goes in the same request form, which takes three attachments. None of it is compulsory beyond the form’s own required fields, but each one removes a round of questions.

SendWhy it helps
The number, exactly as printedIncluding any prefix, suffix or revision letter. Transcribe it rather than tidy it up; a character that looks like noise is sometimes the revision.
A photograph of the label, and of the partWorth more than a description. Labels carry the builder’s number, the component maker’s number, a serial and sometimes a date, and a photograph shows all of them at once without anyone deciding which matters.
What it sits inMachine manufacturer, model and serial number, and where in the machine the part lives. The same part number can appear in several machines with different firmware or different wiring.
How many, and whyOne, one plus a spare, or a quantity for the shelf. And whether the existing part has failed, is suspected, or is being bought ahead of time. It changes what we look for.
Whether the machine is still supportedIf the builder has stopped offering parts, or the company no longer exists, say so. That is the situation this route was built for and it is not a problem to admit to.

The four answers you can get

An evaluation ends in one of four places. Naming them in advance is fairer than letting someone assume the first one is the only good outcome, because on older equipment it frequently is not.

The part is still offered

Then this is a short conversation. We will say so, and tell you whether it comes from the machine builder or the component maker, because those are often two different companies and two different numbers for the same object. Knowing which number you are holding is frequently the whole problem.

The part is no longer offered, but the assembly is repairable

A board, a driver, a power supply or a controller can often be repaired at component level when no exchange unit exists. We evaluate it as received and tell you what condition it is in and what returning it to service involves, as a written scope with a price, before anything is agreed.

A substitute will do the job

Where the original is gone, a part that matches the interfaces and the performance the machine needs can take its place. Every difference from the original is written down, so whoever opens the machine in five years knows what is in there and why.

The part is the wrong question

Sometimes the part someone asks for is not what has failed, or the subsystem around it is at the end of its life and replacing one component buys a month. We would rather say that than sell a part twice. Where a retrofit is the better value we quote it against the repair and let you compare.

How we quote, and what independence means here

We are an independent service provider rather than a distributor, which is why we can compare repair, a substitute and a replacement on their merits instead of recommending whichever one we happen to hold. What we quote arrives as a written scope with a price and terms, once we know what the part is: the specific part decides availability and timing, so those come with the quote rather than ahead of it.

Where equipment is under an active manufacturer warranty, work is only carried out within a scope authorised in writing by the seller or the manufacturer, because doing otherwise can end the warranty. If you are in that position, tell us and we will say where the line is.
