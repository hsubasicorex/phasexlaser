Context

A 2000-unit OPA installed base, thirty years deep

Light Conversion is a Lithuanian manufacturer and one of the most successful suppliers of tunable ultrafast sources to academic research anywhere. The company states over 2000 TOPAS units installed worldwide and around 10,000 femtosecond systems in the field, and says its lasers are used for scientific research at all of the world's top fifty universities.

Two facts from the company's own published history matter for service. The TOPAS dates to the mid 1990s, and the US sales and service center opened in 2006. So roughly a decade of US TOPAS deliveries arrived before there was any US service presence at all, and the earliest units in American laboratories are now around thirty years old.

The current catalog position is also worth stating plainly, because it surprises laboratories. Light Conversion's Ti:Sapphire-pumped OPA line now lists three products: TOPAS-PRIME, TOPAS-PRIME-HE and TOPAS-TWINS. The generation that most of that 2000-unit base belongs to, including TOPAS-C, TOPAS-800, TOPAS-white and TOPAS-SHBC, is not among the current products.

Stated carefully

We have not found a published end-of-support notice for the earlier TOPAS generation. Catalog absence is an indicator, not a manufacturer statement. Before assuming either way, ask Light Conversion directly what they will still do for your serial number. If the answer does not solve your problem, that is the point at which an independent evaluation becomes useful.

Where the North American service footprint sits

Light Conversion lists one authorized service center for North America, in Bozeman, Montana. For a laboratory on the east coast that is a long way from the bench, and it is one office covering a continent. We are in Parsippany, New Jersey.

The pump comes first, every time

This is the most useful thing we can tell a laboratory with a Light Conversion OPA problem, and it saves shipping costs more often than any other single point on this site.

The test

Measure the pump at the module input: energy or average power, pulse duration, beam quality, and timing relative to the seed.

A parametric module will collapse if any one of those four has drifted, with nothing at all wrong inside the module. Until the pump has been characterized at the module input, no statement about the crystal, the stages or the tuning is worth making.

In practice a TOPAS or ORPHEUS is pumped by a Ti:Sapphire amplifier or by a PHAROS or CARBIDE, and the pump is where the fault usually is. An amplifier that has lost pulse energy, or whose compressor has drifted and lengthened the pump pulse, or whose beam quality has degraded, will present at the experiment as an OPA problem. We see that repeatedly.

The full treatment is on the OPO and OPA page, and the pump side is covered on the femtosecond laser page.

Where these systems fail

01

Pump condition at the module input

Energy, pulse duration, beam quality or timing. First on the list, and responsible for a large share of what arrives diagnosed as module failure.

02

White-light generation stage

In TOPAS and ORPHEUS designs, the continuum generation medium and the focus into it. A degraded or damaged generation medium removes the seed, and therefore the output.

03

Seed and pump overlap and the delay line

Spatial and temporal overlap, and the mechanics that set it. Adjustable, recoverable, and among the most common real faults inside the module.

04

Phase-matching condition

Crystal angle or temperature, and whether the working condition can still be reached across the intended range. Frequently recoverable by mapping rather than by replacement.

05

Motorized stages, encoders and home references

Stages that will not move, move without tracking readback, or have lost their home position. Cheap to fix, and a common cause of apparent tuning-range loss.

06

Calibration tables and the software path

On these modules the wavelength request is mapped onto hardware positions by a calibration table. A corrupted or lost table presents as a tuning fault with entirely healthy hardware.

07

Crystal and optic condition

Surface and bulk damage, coating degradation, and contamination on crystal faces and separation optics. Genuine, and less common than assumed.

08

Harmonic and extension modules

Where a NirUVis, DFG or SH or FH extension is fitted, its own crystals, ovens and separation optics, and whether the fundamental reaching it is healthy.

09

Oven and thermal control

Crystal ovens, TEC loops, sensors and drivers in temperature-tuned designs.

10

Controller, supply and interface electronics

Controllers, stage drivers, supplies, communication interfaces and the control software path including LabVIEW and MATLAB integrations.

How an evaluation runs

Every job runs on a written scope. The order below exists to eliminate a branch cheaply before the next, more invasive step is justified.

  1. As-received condition report. Photographed and recorded before any setting, stage position or alignment is changed.
  2. Configuration verification. Every module label read separately, including the pump source and any harmonic or extension module.
  3. Pump characterization at the module input. Energy or average power, pulse duration, spectrum, beam profile and timing. This is the gate for everything that follows.
  4. Infrastructure and control check. Supplies, cooling, interlocks, communication, and whether the wavelength request path actually reaches the hardware.
  5. Calibration integrity check. Whether stages track their readback and whether the calibration table is intact.
  6. Seed and overlap assessment. White-light generation condition, and spatial and temporal overlap with the pump.
  7. Tuning map. Output measured against wavelength across the full intended range, with the spectrum recorded rather than only the power.
  8. Phase-matching map. Output against crystal angle or temperature, which distinguishes a lost working condition from a damaged crystal.
  9. Optics inspection. Crystal faces, coatings, generation medium and separation optics under magnification.
  10. Written findings. What was measured, what was found, what remains unknown, and the repair options with their risks and cost.
  11. Repair on written approval only. Within the approved scope. Deviations require a new approval.
  12. Validation. Tuning range, output against wavelength, spectrum and stability, with a written service report.

Required shipment components

Parametric modules are harder to evaluate in isolation than any other subsystem, because their behavior is defined by their pump. Wherever it is possible, send the pump with the module.

  • The parametric module, complete, including any harmonic or extension modules normally used with it
  • The controller and power supply
  • All interconnecting cables, including trigger and synchronization cables
  • The pump source, if it can be released. A module evaluated against a substitute pump can be characterized, but it cannot be validated against the behavior you see in your own laboratory
  • Control software and calibration files. On these modules the calibration table is part of the instrument
  • Manuals, configuration sheets, acceptance data and prior service reports, however incomplete

If the pump cannot be released, tell us at the enquiry stage. We will say whether a module-only evaluation answers your question, and where it does not we will tell you that instead of taking the shipment.

Limits and safety

What we will not claim

  • We are not a factory-authorized service center for any manufacturer named on this page.
  • We do not guarantee a repair before evaluation. A finding that a system is not economically repairable is a legitimate result and is delivered in writing.
  • We do not perform manufacturer-equivalent calibration, and we do not certify to original published specifications without the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards.
  • We do not claim service experience on a configuration we have not actually worked on.

Warranty

Manufacturer warranties in this industry generally void coverage on a unit serviced by a party the manufacturer has not authorized. If your system is inside its warranty period, use the manufacturer, or obtain written authorization from the seller before engaging us. Our addressable work is out-of-warranty and legacy equipment, which is where the support gap sits in any case.

Laser safety

These are Class 4 systems with tunable output. Eyewear selected for the pump wavelength does not protect against a signal or idler wavelength, and much of the output range is invisible. Bench work is performed in a controlled area with beam containment, eyewear appropriate to every wavelength the system can produce rather than only the one it is set to, defined and terminated paths for signal, idler and residual pump, and access control.
