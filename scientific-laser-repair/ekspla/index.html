Context

A capable manufacturer, and a service desk eight time zones away

EKSPLA is a Lithuanian manufacturer of picosecond and nanosecond lasers and tunable parametric systems, with a substantial installed base in US universities and national laboratories. The equipment is well regarded. The service geometry is the problem a US laboratory runs into.

From EKSPLA's own published support information, the scientific service department is in Vilnius, with a Lithuanian telephone number and stated hours of 08:30 to 16:30 in the GMT+2 time zone, and a service department address on Savanoriu Avenue in Vilnius. The published contact for the US branch is an orders address rather than a service contact.

For a laboratory on US Eastern time, the practical consequence is that the manufacturer's support window has closed before the American working day starts, and a hardware repair points back across the Atlantic. Crating a laser to Lithuania is expensive, slow and exposed to customs on both legs. That is the gap this page addresses.

Stated carefully

This is a description of published contact information, not a criticism of the manufacturer's competence or willingness. Where EKSPLA can solve your problem, use EKSPLA. Independent evaluation becomes useful when the system is out of warranty and the logistics or the timeline do not work for your experiment.

Where these systems fail

01

Pump condition at the module input

On the NT tunable and PG parametric families, the first measurement. A parametric section cannot exceed what the pump delivers, and pump energy, pulse duration, beam quality and timing all matter at once.

02

Seed diodes and high-speed seed drivers

On the seeded picosecond platforms, the driver that defines pulse amplitude and timing. A failure here produces a system that is electrically and thermally perfect and optically dead.

03

Pump diodes and pump current controllers

Driver failures, protection trips and degraded diodes, frequently silent, with the control layer reporting no error while holding a limited state.

04

Amplifier chain and its interfaces

Gain stages and the optics and fiber interfaces between them. Damage at a single interface removes the output with no electrical trace.

05

Q-switch and driver on the nanosecond platforms

Pockels cell assembly, driver and timing, and hold-off behavior.

06

Harmonic and parametric modules

Crystals, ovens, temperature control and separation optics, assessed only after the fundamental reaching each stage has been measured.

07

Motorized tuning stages and calibration

On NT and PG systems, stages that will not move or no longer track readback, and calibration tables that map a requested wavelength onto hardware positions. A lost table presents as a tuning fault with healthy hardware.

08

Thermal control

TEC loops, sensors, drivers, chillers and flow. Presents as a silent power limit far more often than as a temperature alarm.

09

Power-monitor and feedback paths

Monitor photodiodes, conditioning, converter stages and the control loop, in both failure directions.

10

Controller, communication and software path

Controllers, serial and Ethernet interfaces, and the control software including LabVIEW and MATLAB integrations.

How an evaluation runs

Every job runs on a written scope. The order below exists to eliminate a branch cheaply before the next, more invasive step is justified.

  1. As-received condition report. Photographed and recorded before any setting or connection is changed.
  2. Configuration verification. Every module label read separately. What the system is takes precedence over what the record says it is.
  3. Infrastructure check. Supplies, rails, cooling, interlocks and their states, control communication, and whether readback is sensed or echoed.
  4. Measurement setup elimination. Noise floor established. Output measured by wavelength with calibrated free-space detection and wavelength-selective optics on a defined, terminated beam path.
  5. Pulse-train verification. Fast photodetection and high-bandwidth acquisition to establish whether pulses exist, at what rate, and in what relationship to the trigger.
  6. Stage-by-stage measurement. Power and beam condition measured at the interfaces between subsystems.
  7. Command-path measurement. Commanded values compared against actual drive current at the hardware.
  8. Fault isolation. The chain is bisected until the fault sits in one subsystem, then in one component or interface where that is possible.
  9. Written findings. What was measured, what was found, what remains unknown, and the repair options with their risks and cost.
  10. Repair on written approval only. Within the approved scope. Deviations require a new approval.
  11. Validation. Electrical, optical, timing and functional validation, with a written service report.

Autocorrelation, pulse-width and M² measurements are performed through owned equipment or a qualified outside laboratory depending on the system and the question being answered. The evaluation proposal states which measurements will be made, by what method, and which are sourced externally.

Required shipment components

A system that cannot be brought to its normal operating state on the bench cannot be diagnosed. For bench evaluation we normally need:

  • Laser head
  • Controller
  • Power supply
  • All interconnecting cables, including any with a non-standard pinout
  • Any dedicated module required for normal operation, including conversion, trigger, pump or paired units
  • Control software, license files and interface documentation, including a MATLAB or LabVIEW control path where that is how the system is driven
  • Manuals, configuration sheets, acceptance data and prior service reports, however incomplete

On discontinued platforms the customer's own paperwork is frequently the only surviving record of the configuration. Send it even when it looks irrelevant.

International shipments

We receive systems from outside the United States, including from Canadian universities and research institutes. We provide the commercial documentation a repair shipment normally requires and coordinate with your shipping office. Customs classification, duties, temporary import treatment and clearance timing remain the sender's responsibility, and we do not give customs guarantees. Insurance is the sender's decision and should reflect replacement value. We document the as-received condition on arrival, which is the evidence you would need if transit damage has occurred.

Limits and safety

What we will not claim

  • We are not a factory-authorized service center for any manufacturer named on this page.
  • We do not guarantee a repair before evaluation. A finding that a system is not economically repairable is a legitimate result and is delivered in writing.
  • We do not perform manufacturer-equivalent calibration, and we do not certify to original published specifications without the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards.
  • We do not claim service experience on a configuration we have not actually worked on.

Warranty

Manufacturer warranties in this industry generally void coverage on a unit serviced by a party the manufacturer has not authorized. If your system is inside its warranty period, use the manufacturer, or obtain written authorization from the seller before engaging us. Our addressable work is out-of-warranty and legacy equipment, which is where the support gap sits in any case.

Laser safety

These are Class 4 systems, and the tunable platforms produce output across a wide and frequently invisible range. Eyewear selected for the pump wavelength does not protect against a signal or idler wavelength. Bench work is performed in a controlled area with beam containment, eyewear appropriate to every wavelength the system can produce, defined and terminated beam paths for every output, and access control.
