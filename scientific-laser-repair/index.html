Who this is for

Laboratories that have run out of manufacturer options

Scientific lasers do not fail the way production equipment fails. The system is frequently one of a handful ever built in that configuration, the original engineer has moved on, the manufacturer has been acquired twice, and the model has been declared obsolete. Meanwhile a graduate student has a measurement schedule and an instrument that produces no usable output.

That is the situation PhaseX is built for. We work with:

  • Universities and research laboratories holding instruments bought under grant funding, now outside any service agreement
  • Imaging and microscopy groups running excitation and depletion sources that are no longer catalog products
  • Spectroscopy, ultrafast and attosecond groups whose amplifier and parametric chains are older than the students using them
  • Fluid dynamics and PIV groups whose laser heads were supplied inside somebody else's instrument
  • OEMs and photonics integrators who need a subsystem diagnosed independently before deciding whether to repair, replace or redesign
  • Facilities with imported systems whose manufacturer has no practical service presence in North America
  • Laboratories with orphaned inventory, where a working spare or a documented failure analysis is worth more than a replacement quotation

Context

Why this gap exists

Independent service capacity in North America grew up around the technologies of the 1970s to the 1990s. Ion lasers, dye lasers and lamp-pumped Nd:YAG have had specialist independent support for decades. Modern ultrafast oscillators, amplifiers, parametric modules and scientific fiber platforms largely have not, and for several widely installed families the practical route for a US laboratory has been the original manufacturer and nothing else.

At the same time the manufacturers consolidated. A platform designed by a small specialist firm was acquired, the acquirer was acquired, product lines were rationalized, and configuration records did not always survive the transitions. Several of the platforms on this site have changed owners twice since they were installed, and in at least one case the current owner has published a campaign steering the installed base toward replacement rather than repair.

Two structural facts follow from that, and they are the reason this operation exists. The equipment is repairable, because the failures concentrate in electronics, thermal control, mechanics and interfaces rather than in exotic assemblies. And the equipment is frequently irreplaceable in its specific experiment, because the current product is a different architecture with different wavelength coverage and different pulse characteristics.

What this is not

This is not a claim to be the only option, and it is not a criticism of any manufacturer's competence. Where the manufacturer can solve your problem, use the manufacturer. Independent evaluation becomes useful when the system is out of warranty and the answer, the logistics or the timeline do not work for your experiment.

What the service covers

The service is diagnostic engineering first. Repair follows a written finding, not a guess.

01

Incoming inspection and as-found reporting

Condition, labels, configuration and shipping damage recorded before any setting is changed. The as-received state is the reference for everything that follows.

02

Electrical and electronic diagnostics

Supplies, rails, sequencing, protection states and control communication. Whether a command actually reaches the lower-level controller, and whether the readback reflects a physical measurement or a stored value.

03

Diode and driver diagnostics

Seed diodes, high-speed seed drivers, pump diodes, pump-current drivers, Pockels cell drivers and their timing, including behavior under commanded setpoint changes.

04

Seed, oscillator and amplifier troubleshooting

Locating the point in the chain where optical power stops being produced or stops being transmitted, rather than treating the last stage as the cause.

05

Fiber path inspection and loss localization

Connector and end-face inspection, splice evaluation, pump combiner and isolator assessment, and identification of damage points along the optical path.

06

Thermal and cooling systems

TEC modules and drivers, chillers, fans, temperature sensors, crystal ovens and cooling loops, including deionized water quality on flashlamp platforms.

07

Interlock, shutter, trigger and synchronization

Silent protection states, master and slave trigger paths, external trigger inputs, delay behavior, inter-cavity timing and remote-control faults.

08

Wavelength-conversion assessment

SHG, THG, OPO and OPA subsystem evaluation: crystal condition, oven and temperature control, polarization, alignment and conversion efficiency against the fundamental actually arriving at the stage.

09

Optical measurement

Output power and pulse energy by wavelength, pulse-train verification, spectral verification, repetition rate, beam profile and stability behavior.

10

Obsolete component substitution and retrofit engineering

Substitutes, redesigned drivers, mounts, cables and interfaces where an original part no longer exists. Only within an approved scope, and always documented as a deviation from original.

11

Post-repair validation and written reporting

Electrical, optical, timing and functional validation, with a written report you can attach to the instrument record.

The evaluation process

Every job runs on a written scope. That is the same rule we apply to industrial machinery, and it matters more here, because a scientific laser can be made permanently worse by an enthusiastic first afternoon.

  1. Intake. You send the model and serial label photographs, system photographs, operating parameters, symptoms, and any measurement or service history. We tell you whether the configuration is one we can usefully evaluate.
  2. Evaluation proposal. A written diagnostic scope with a fixed diagnostic fee, the tests to be performed, the expected turnaround, and what a negative result would mean.
  3. As-received condition report. Photographed and recorded before any setting or connection is changed.
  4. Bring-up. Supplies, cooling, interlocks and control communication verified. Commanded values compared against readback and against physical measurement.
  5. Optical measurement. Output measured separately by wavelength with wavelength-selective optics and calibrated free-space detection. The actual optical pulse train verified with suitable fast photodetection.
  6. Fault localization. The chain is divided until the fault is isolated to a subsystem, then to a component or an interface where that is possible.
  7. Written findings. What was measured, what was found, what is unknown and why, and the repair options with their risks.
  8. Your decision. No repair work begins without your written approval of the scope and cost.
  9. Repair or retrofit. Within the approved scope only. Any deviation requires a new approval.
  10. Validation and return. Electrical, optical, timing and functional validation, a written service report, and documented packing for return shipment.
On honest outcomes

Some evaluations end with a finding that the system is not economically repairable, or that the failed part is a custom assembly with no available substitute. That is a legitimate result of a diagnostic engagement, and you receive the same written documentation. A failure analysis that lets a laboratory justify a replacement purchase has value of its own.

Required shipment components

A system that cannot be brought to its normal operating state on the bench cannot be diagnosed. For bench evaluation we normally need:

  • Laser head
  • Controller
  • Power supply
  • All interconnecting cables, including any with a non-standard pinout
  • Any dedicated module required for normal operation, including conversion, trigger, pump or paired units
  • Control software, license files and interface documentation, including a MATLAB or LabVIEW control path where that is how the system is driven
  • Manuals, configuration sheets, acceptance data and prior service reports, however incomplete

On discontinued platforms the customer's own paperwork is frequently the only surviving record of the configuration. Send it even when it looks irrelevant.

International shipments

We receive systems from outside the United States, including from Canadian universities and research institutes. We provide the commercial documentation a repair shipment normally requires and coordinate with your shipping office. Customs classification, duties, temporary import treatment and clearance timing remain the sender's responsibility, and we do not give customs guarantees. Insurance is the sender's decision and should reflect replacement value. We document the as-received condition on arrival, which is the evidence you would need if transit damage has occurred.

Limits and safety

What we will not claim

  • We are not a factory-authorized service center for any manufacturer named on this page.
  • We do not guarantee a repair before evaluation. A finding that a system is not economically repairable is a legitimate result and is delivered in writing.
  • We do not perform manufacturer-equivalent calibration, and we do not certify to original published specifications without the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards.
  • We do not claim service experience on a configuration we have not actually worked on.

Warranty

Manufacturer warranties in this industry generally void coverage on a unit serviced by a party the manufacturer has not authorized. If your system is inside its warranty period, use the manufacturer, or obtain written authorization from the seller before engaging us. Our addressable work is out-of-warranty and legacy equipment, which is where the support gap sits in any case.

Laser safety

These are Class 4 systems. Bench work is performed in a controlled area with beam containment, eyewear appropriate to every wavelength present rather than only the expected one, defined and terminated beam paths, and access control. Where the configuration is not yet verified, the area is prepared for the widest plausible wavelength range until the actual output has been measured.

Common questions

Do you service lasers the manufacturer no longer supports?

Discontinued and end-of-support systems are the main reason laboratories contact us. Faults concentrate in drivers, supplies, thermal control, feedback paths, fiber interfaces, connectors, mechanics and control electronics, all of which respond to component-level engineering. What ends a repair is a failed custom assembly with no available substitute, not a catalog status.

Will you quote a repair before seeing the laser?

No. A fixed repair price before the fault is located would be a guess. We quote a defined diagnostic evaluation with a fixed fee, report the findings in writing, and only then quote the repair scope for your approval.

Do you take systems from outside the United States?

Yes, including from Canadian universities and research institutes. We provide the commercial documentation a repair shipment normally requires and coordinate with your shipping office. Customs classification, duties, temporary import treatment and clearance timing remain the sender's responsibility, and we do not give customs guarantees.

Can you certify a repaired laser to its original specifications?

Not where the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards are unavailable, because there would be nothing to certify against. What we document is the measured condition on arrival, the fault found, the work performed, and the measured condition on completion.

What does an evaluation cost?

A fixed diagnostic fee, quoted in writing for your specific system before anything ships, and stated in the evaluation proposal alongside the tests it covers. We do not quote a diagnostic fee on a system whose configuration we have not yet been able to establish, because the fee depends on what has to be measured.

Next step

The first question is always whether the configuration on your bench is the configuration on the label. On discontinued and OEM systems it frequently is not, and that changes the entire diagnostic approach. Start with photographs and the measured behavior.
