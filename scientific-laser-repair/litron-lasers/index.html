Context

A widely installed laser with no US company behind it

Litron Lasers is a UK manufacturer of flashlamp-pumped and diode-pumped nanosecond Nd:YAG systems. Their lasers are common in US laboratories, and a substantial part of that installed base arrived inside somebody else's product.

The PIV problem

Litron supplies dual-cavity laser heads that are integrated into particle image velocimetry systems sold under other brands. A fluid dynamics group can therefore run a Litron laser for a decade without the name ever appearing in front of the operator, and only discover it when the system stops firing and the integrator's support points at the laser head. If your PIV laser head carries a Litron label, or your documentation references a Nano or LPY model, this page is about your system.

The US footprint

On Litron's own published distributor listing, the entry for the USA is the company head office in Rugby, Warwickshire, England. Their service-centre information names a single US service contractor, in Cranston, Rhode Island, and their RMA policy sets out return shipment to England as the route. There is no US manufacturing or service subsidiary in that published material.

For a laboratory with a working experiment and an aging laser, that geometry is the problem. A transatlantic repair cycle is expensive, slow and exposed to customs twice. We are in Parsippany, New Jersey.

Stated carefully

This is a description of published contact and distribution information, not a criticism of the manufacturer or of their US contractor. Where Litron or their appointed service centre can solve your problem, use them. Independent evaluation becomes useful when the system is out of warranty and the logistics or the timeline do not work for your experiment.

Where these systems fail

01

Flashlamps

Consumable by design. Aging presents as falling energy, rising trigger requirement, and eventually failure to fire.

02

Laser rods, mounts and seals

Rod and coating condition, and the mount and seal holding it in the flow path.

03

Flow tubes, seals and O-rings

A classic aging failure, and a common cause of coolant where coolant should not be.

04

Cooling loop and water quality

Flow, heat exchanger, filters, resin and conductivity. Presents as falling energy and thermal instability rather than as an obvious leak.

05

PFN and high-voltage supply

Pulse-forming network and HV charging supply. Capacitor aging is routine at these ages, and this is repairable electronics.

06

Q-switch and driver

Pockels cell assembly, driver, timing and hold-off behavior. Determines whether you get a Q-switched pulse at all.

07

Dual-cavity synchronization on PIV heads

The timing relationship between the two cavities and the trigger chain that sets the pulse separation. On a PIV system this is the measurement, so a timing fault is a system-level failure.

08

Harmonic modules

Second and higher harmonic generators, crystals, ovens and separation optics, assessed after the fundamental has been measured.

09

Cavity optics and alignment

Mirrors, apertures and the alignment that sets energy and beam quality.

10

Trigger, interlock and control electronics

Internal and external trigger paths, lamp and Q-switch synchronization, interlocks, shutters, control boards and remote panels.

The parts question, answered honestly

On flashlamp-pumped platforms the parts situation decides whether a repair is possible, so we deal with it before quoting rather than after.

Generally obtainable

  • Flashlamps for common configurations, from third-party suppliers
  • Laser rods for common configurations
  • Flow tubes, seals, O-rings and cooling-loop consumables
  • Generic electronic components for PFN, supply and control board repair
  • Optics that are standard cuts and coatings

Checked case by case, and not promised in advance

  • PFN assemblies and capacitors of specific values and form factors
  • Q-switch drivers and high-voltage switching assemblies
  • High-voltage charging supplies as complete units
  • Harmonic crystals of specific cuts and coatings
  • Custom control boards, and OEM-specific interface boards fitted for an integrator's system
  • Non-standard cables and connectors, which we can usually rebuild once the pinout is established
How we handle it

The evaluation establishes what your system needs. We then check availability for those specific parts and tell you in writing what can be obtained, what would have to be repaired rather than replaced, what would have to be engineered, and what cannot be sourced at all.

How an evaluation runs

Every job runs on a written scope. The order below exists to eliminate a branch cheaply before the next, more invasive step is justified.

  1. As-received condition report. Photographed and recorded before anything is changed, including the cooling loop state.
  2. Configuration verification. Every label read, including any integrator's badging, and the PFN and control electronics generation established.
  3. Cooling and infrastructure check. Flow, heat exchanger, filters, water quality and leak inspection.
  4. Electrical safety and HV integrity check. Before the system is charged, the HV path, interlocks and protection are verified.
  5. Lamp and PFN assessment. Trigger behavior, charging behavior, lamp condition and PFN integrity, per cavity on a dual-cavity head.
  6. Fundamental energy measurement. Per cavity, on a defined and terminated path.
  7. Q-switch and timing assessment. Pulse duration, hold-off, and on PIV heads the inter-cavity timing and jitter.
  8. Harmonic module assessment. Only after the fundamental reaching each stage has been measured.
  9. Parts availability check. For the specific items your system needs, before any repair is quoted.
  10. Written findings. What was measured, what was found, the parts situation, and the repair options with their risks and cost.
  11. Repair on written approval only. Within the approved scope.
  12. Validation. Energy per cavity, pulse duration, timing and jitter, beam condition and stability, with a written service report.

Required shipment components

A system that cannot be brought to its normal operating state on the bench cannot be diagnosed. For bench evaluation we normally need:

  • Laser head
  • Controller
  • Power supply
  • All interconnecting cables, including any with a non-standard pinout
  • Any dedicated module required for normal operation, including conversion, trigger, pump or paired units
  • Control software, license files and interface documentation, including a MATLAB or LabVIEW control path where that is how the system is driven
  • Manuals, configuration sheets, acceptance data and prior service reports, however incomplete

On discontinued platforms the customer's own paperwork is frequently the only surviving record of the configuration. Send it even when it looks irrelevant.

International shipments

We receive systems from outside the United States, including from Canadian universities and research institutes. We provide the commercial documentation a repair shipment normally requires and coordinate with your shipping office. Customs classification, duties, temporary import treatment and clearance timing remain the sender's responsibility, and we do not give customs guarantees. Insurance is the sender's decision and should reflect replacement value. We document the as-received condition on arrival, which is the evidence you would need if transit damage has occurred.

Limits and safety

What we will not claim

  • We are not a factory-authorized service center for any manufacturer named on this page.
  • We do not guarantee a repair before evaluation. A finding that a system is not economically repairable is a legitimate result and is delivered in writing.
  • We do not perform manufacturer-equivalent calibration, and we do not certify to original published specifications without the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards.
  • We do not claim service experience on a configuration we have not actually worked on.

Warranty

Manufacturer warranties in this industry generally void coverage on a unit serviced by a party the manufacturer has not authorized. If your system is inside its warranty period, use the manufacturer, or obtain written authorization from the seller before engaging us. Our addressable work is out-of-warranty and legacy equipment, which is where the support gap sits in any case.

Laser safety

These are Class 4 systems, and flashlamp-pumped nanosecond platforms add stored electrical energy at lethal voltages to the optical hazard. PFN capacitors hold charge after shutdown. Bench work is performed in a controlled area with beam containment, eyewear appropriate to the fundamental and every harmonic present, defined and terminated beam paths, access control, and high-voltage safety practice including verified discharge before any access to the PFN or supply.
