One measurement comes before everything else

Optical parametric oscillators and amplifiers convert pump light into tunable signal and idler light. They are not sources in their own right. Everything they do is bounded by what the pump delivers, and unlike a simple frequency-doubling stage they are sensitive to four separate pump properties at once.

The test

Measure the pump at the module input: energy or average power, pulse duration, beam quality, and timing relative to the seed or the cavity.

A parametric device will collapse if any one of those four has drifted, with nothing at all wrong inside the module. Until the pump has been characterized at the module input, no statement about the crystal, the cavity or the tuning hardware is worth making.

This is the single most common reason a laboratory sends us an OPA that turns out not to be the problem. The amplifier feeding it had lost pulse energy, or its compressor had drifted and lengthened the pump pulse, or its beam quality had degraded. In each case the parametric module was healthy and the repair belonged upstream.

The related case for simple harmonic stages is set out on the SHG and harmonic generation page. The principle is the same and the sensitivity here is higher.

OPO and OPA are different machines

The two get discussed together and they fail differently. Knowing which one you have changes the diagnostic order.

OPOOPA
ArchitectureA resonant cavity around the nonlinear crystal, oscillating from parametric gainA single or multi-pass amplifier seeded by a white-light continuum or a separate seed
What can go wrong uniquelyCavity alignment, cavity length and synchronization to the pump, cavity optic conditionSeed generation, seed and pump spatial and temporal overlap, delay-line position and stability
Typical failure signatureWill not oscillate, threshold has risen, output unstable or drops out at specific wavelengthsOutput collapsed, gain low, tuning range narrowed, output present but unstable pulse to pulse
Sensitivity to pump timingCavity length must match the pump repetition rate in synchronously pumped designsDelay between seed and pump must be correct to picosecond or better
Common cheap fixCavity length and alignment recoveryDelay-line and overlap recovery

Many practical systems combine both, and multi-stage commercial modules frequently contain white-light generation, a preamplifier, a power amplifier and one or more harmonic stages in the same box.

Where parametric systems fail

01

Pump condition at the module input

Energy, pulse duration, beam quality or timing. First on the list and responsible for a large share of what gets diagnosed as module failure.

02

White-light or seed generation

In OPA designs, the continuum generation stage. A degraded or damaged generation medium, or a drifted focus into it, removes the seed and therefore the output.

03

Seed and pump overlap

Spatial and temporal overlap, and the delay line that sets it. Mechanical, adjustable, and among the most recoverable faults on this list.

04

Phase-matching condition

Crystal angle or temperature, and whether the correct condition can still be reached across the intended range. Recoverable by mapping rather than by replacement in many cases.

05

Motorized tuning stages and encoders

Stages that will not move, move without tracking readback, or have lost their home reference. Cheap to fix and a common cause of apparent tuning-range loss.

06

Crystal condition

Surface and bulk damage, and coating degradation. Genuine, and less common than assumed.

07

Cavity optics and alignment in an OPO

Mirror condition, cavity length, and synchronization to the pump repetition rate.

08

Separation and delivery optics

Dichroics, filters and beam dumps that separate signal, idler and residual pump. A degraded separator removes the light you are trying to measure.

09

Thermal and oven control

Crystal ovens, TEC loops, sensors and drivers in temperature-tuned designs.

10

Control electronics and software path

Controllers, communication, calibration tables and the software that maps a requested wavelength onto hardware positions. A corrupted or lost calibration table presents as a tuning fault with healthy hardware.

How an evaluation runs

  1. As-received condition report. Photographed and recorded before any setting, position or alignment is changed.
  2. Configuration verification. Every module label read separately, including the pump source and any harmonic or extension modules.
  3. Pump characterization at the module input. Energy or average power, pulse duration, spectrum, beam profile and timing. This is the gate for everything that follows.
  4. Infrastructure check. Supplies, cooling, interlocks, communication, and whether readback values are sensed or echoed.
  5. Control and calibration check. Whether the wavelength request path reaches the hardware, whether stages track their readback, and whether the calibration table is intact.
  6. Seed or cavity assessment. White-light generation and overlap in an OPA; cavity alignment, length and synchronization in an OPO.
  7. Tuning map. Output measured against wavelength across the full intended range, with the spectrum recorded, not only the power.
  8. Phase-matching map. Output against crystal angle or temperature, which distinguishes a lost working condition from a damaged crystal.
  9. Optics inspection. Crystal faces, coatings, generation medium, cavity optics and separation optics under magnification.
  10. Written findings. What was measured, what was found, what remains unknown, and the repair options with their risks and cost.
  11. Repair on written approval only. Within the approved scope. Deviations require a new approval.
  12. Validation. Tuning range, output against wavelength, spectrum and stability, with a written service report.
On restoring a tuning range

We do not promise restoration of a tuning range we cannot verify against original acceptance data. Where that data exists we work to it. Where it does not, we document the range and output achieved on completion and compare it against the range the system produced on arrival. That is an honest record and it is the one your instrument file can actually use.

Before you assume coverage

Contact us to confirm your configuration. On parametric modules the crystal set, the stage hardware, the calibration path and the pump pairing matter more than the family name, and we would rather tell you at the enquiry stage that a module is outside what we can usefully evaluate than after you have paid to crate it.

Required shipment components

Parametric modules are harder to evaluate in isolation than any other subsystem, because their behavior is defined by their pump. Wherever it is possible, send the pump with the module.

  • The parametric module, complete, including any harmonic or extension modules normally used with it
  • The controller and power supply
  • All interconnecting cables, including trigger and synchronization cables
  • The pump source, if it can be released. A module evaluated against a substitute pump can be characterized, but it cannot be validated against the behavior you actually see in your laboratory
  • Control software, calibration files and interface documentation. On these systems the calibration table is part of the instrument
  • Manuals, configuration sheets, acceptance data and prior service reports, however incomplete

If the pump cannot be released, tell us at the enquiry stage. We will say whether a module-only evaluation answers your question, and where it does not we will tell you that instead of taking the shipment.

International shipments

We receive systems from outside the United States, including from Canadian universities and research institutes. We provide the commercial documentation a repair shipment normally requires and coordinate with your shipping office. Customs classification, duties, temporary import treatment and clearance timing remain the sender's responsibility, and we do not give customs guarantees. Limits and safety

What we will not claim

  • We are not a factory-authorized service center for any manufacturer named on this page.
  • We do not guarantee a repair before evaluation. A finding that a system is not economically repairable is a legitimate result and is delivered in writing.
  • We do not perform manufacturer-equivalent calibration, and we do not certify to original published specifications without the original acceptance data and the corresponding standards.
  • We do not claim service experience on a configuration we have not actually worked on.

Warranty

Manufacturer warranties in this industry generally void coverage on a unit serviced by a party the manufacturer has not authorized. If your system is inside its warranty period, use the manufacturer, or obtain written authorization from the seller before engaging us. Our addressable work is out-of-warranty and legacy equipment, which is where the support gap sits in any case.

Laser safety

These are Class 4 systems with tunable output, and that changes the safety problem. Eyewear selected for the pump wavelength does not protect against a signal or idler wavelength, and much of the output range is invisible. Bench work is performed in a controlled area with beam containment, eyewear appropriate to every wavelength the system can produce rather than only the one it is set to, defined and terminated beam paths for signal, idler and residual pump, and access control.
