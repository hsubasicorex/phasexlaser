Why this page is organised by machine

An independent service firm earns its place on the machines whose builders have moved on, whose support contracts have lapsed, or whose owners need somebody who will look at the whole system rather than one assembly. That is what independent service exists to do.

The classes below are grouped by what the equipment is, not by the industry that bought it. A welding cell in a battery plant and a welding cell in a job shop share a source, an optic, a fixture and a controller; what differs is the record the owner has to keep, and that record is always theirs.

Equipment classes

Ordered by what a stoppage costs the owner. Each page states what is in scope, what is not, and who owns the result.

01

Semiconductor and wafer laser equipment service

A scribing, dicing or microprocessing tool that has stopped holds up everything downstream of it, and the assembly the quote names is the one nobody is allowed to open. We work the system around the optics: the source interface, the beam path, the stage, the controls, the extraction and the utilities.

02

Battery and e-mobility laser equipment service

Busbar welding, cell and module sealing, tray lines and stator welding run on one hardware stack: a fiber source, programmable optics, a fixture, a sensor interface and a controller. When one of them stops, a line stops with it.

03

Robotic welding, cladding and deposition cell service

A cladding or deposition cell is a welding cell with a feed path added, and the feed path is where most of the frustration lives. We take the cell as a whole: source, head, delivery, motion, shielding and control.

04

Scientific and ultrafast laboratory systems

Ultrafast and femtosecond sources, harmonic stages, OPO and OPA lines, pump drivers, and systems the manufacturer no longer supports.

05

Packaging, converting and plate imaging machinery

Flexo plate imagers, die-cutting and converting lines, coding and marking heads, gravure and screen engraving.

06

Cutting, welding and marking machines

Flatbed and tube cutting machines, welding cells, handheld welders and marking stations, including cleaning systems.

07

Laboratory and production system integration

Benches, enclosures, motion, control and the interfaces between bought-in subsystems.

How a job runs, on any of them

  1. What you send. Machine make, model and serial, what it does now, what changed first, and the fault text exactly as the machine shows it.
  2. Written scope and a fixed evaluation fee. Quoted before anyone travels, including what a negative result would mean.
  3. Evaluation. The machine as found rather than as documented, with instrumentation named in the proposal.
  4. Written findings. What was measured, which subsystem the fault sits in, what remains unknown, and the options with their cost and risk.
  5. Your approval. Nothing is repaired, substituted or modified until you approve the scope and the price in writing.
  6. Work, validation and report. Inside the approved scope, then a written service report you keep.

What we do not take on

We are not an authorized agent of any manufacturer, and we say so on every page. We do not open a sealed or proprietary optical module - it is replaced, or routed back to its maker, and we tell you which before you commit. We do not qualify a process: the weld, the deposit, the cut, the scribe and the cleaned surface are yours to judge against your own records. We do not calibrate metrology hardware, because calibration needs accreditation we do not hold.

Saying this plainly is not modesty. It is the only way a written scope means anything.

On every route: the machine is the deliverable, the process result is the customer's. PhaseX does not qualify a weld, a deposit, a cleaned substrate, a scribe or a cut.
