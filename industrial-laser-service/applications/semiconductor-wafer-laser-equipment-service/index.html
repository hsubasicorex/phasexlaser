What the tool is, as a service object

A wafer-level laser tool is three machines sharing a frame. There is the laser and its optical train, which on most platforms is a module the builder supplies as a sealed unit. There is a precision stage with its own metrology, drives and controller. And there is the plant around both: extraction, cooling, compressed air, vacuum, power conditioning and the safety chain that lets any of it run.

Those three fail differently and they are owned differently. The sealed optical module belongs to its maker - on stealth-dicing platforms it is a laser and optical module developed by a third party under its own patents, and nothing we do opens it. The stage, the controls, the utilities and the interfaces between them are ordinary precision machinery, and that is where an independent service firm is useful.

What gets reported

Owners describe an outcome, not a cause, and that is the right way round. What we hear:

  • the tool faults mid-lot and the log points at an axis, an interlock or a utility rather than the laser
  • throughput has drifted down and nobody changed a recipe
  • a stage no longer repeats, or repeats only when the room is cold
  • extraction alarms, or the enclosure no longer clears
  • an interlock will not reset and the chain is older than the people maintaining it
  • the OEM has quoted a module replacement and the owner wants the rest of the machine assessed before committing
That last case is worth raising early. A module quote is a large number, and whether the machine around it justifies the spend is a different question from whether the module is at fault.

What is in scope, and what is not

In scope: the source interface and its control and power electronics, beam delivery outside the sealed module, the scanner and its drive electronics, the stage and motion system with its feedback and drives, the machine controller and its I/O, the safety chain and interlocks, extraction and filtration, cooling, and the utility interfaces.

Not in scope, and said before a quote rather than after: a sealed or proprietary optical module is not opened. It is replaced, or routed back to its maker, and the written proposal says which. Process qualification - whether the tool is producing acceptable die - belongs to the fab and to nobody else.

How a job runs

An evaluation comes first and it is quoted as a fixed fee before anyone travels. We record the machine and its serial, the configuration as found rather than as documented, what changed before the symptom appeared, and the state of the subsystems listed above.

What you get back is a written report: what was found, what we propose, what we would repair, what we would replace, what we would route elsewhere, and the commercial options with each. You decide. Nothing proceeds on a verbal.

What to send with a request

Enough to answer you properly, and nothing that needs the machine opened.

  • the tool builder and model, and the serial if you have it
  • what the machine was doing when it stopped
  • what changed in the days before - a service visit, a utility interruption, a move, a recipe or a consumable
  • the fault text or code exactly as the machine shows it
  • photographs of the machine, the enclosure and the rear panels
  • any OEM quotation you already hold

Who owns what

A proprietary or closed optical module is not opened. Source interface, cooling, motion, control, safety, extraction, utilities and peripherals are in scope; the sealed module is replaced or routed to the OEM.

Laser safety on your site

Work is carried out to the site rules and the laser safety arrangements in force at your facility. The Laser Safety Officer is designated by the employer, never by PhaseX; hazard classification, nominal hazard zone determination and any compliance opinion stay with your own officer.

Questions owners ask

Will you open the laser module inside our dicing tool?

No. On stealth-dicing and similar platforms the laser and optical module is a sealed unit built by a third party under its own patents, and we do not open it. What we do is establish whether the module is actually the problem, and service everything around it: the stage, the drives, the controller, the safety chain, the extraction and the utilities. If the module is at fault we say so, and it is replaced or routed back to its maker.

Can you work inside our cleanroom?

That is your call and your protocol, not ours. We work to the access rules, gowning and qualification requirements the facility sets, and where those cannot be met we work on subassemblies removed to a bench. What we will not do is claim a cleanroom qualification we do not hold.

The OEM quoted a replacement. Can you tell us whether it is worth it?

That is the question an independent evaluation exists to answer. We evaluate the machine around the quoted assembly - the stage, the controls, the utilities, the general condition - and give you a written view of what the rest of the tool has left in it. The decision stays yours and we have no interest in the outcome either way.

Do you service lithography tools?

No. Lithography and EUV platforms are under total OEM service control, and we decline that work rather than stretch a scope to cover it.
