The machine, as distinct from the laser

Sheet metal cutters, tube and profile machines, combination machines, coil-fed lines and plate gantries all sit here, and so do the CO2 cutting machines still earning their keep. It is worth separating machine from laser because owners and suppliers routinely conflate them. The machine is the frame, the motion system and its drives, the CNC and the control platform, the table or exchanger, the assist gas path and regulation, the extraction and filtration, the chiller loops, the enclosure and the safety circuit. The laser is the source and what carries its light to the work. A machine can be perfectly healthy while the laser is not, and it can equally be the machine that has failed while the laser is sound.

“The laser is not cutting through” is the sentence we hear most, and it is a machine question as often as a laser one: assist gas, focus and height control, motion, or a chiller fault holding the source in derate can all produce it. That is the first question of any service call: is this a laser problem or a machine problem? The answer changes who should be on site, what should ship, and what a realistic repair costs. It is also the question that gets skipped when an owner is handed a quote for a new source.

Machine down, motion faults and cut quality problems

These are the descriptions we are given. Each one is produced by more than one cause, so none of them identifies a fault on its own. They are the right way to start a request, and every one of them is something you can see without opening anything.

  • Needs more passes, slower feed or higher gas than it used to
  • Cut edge dragging, dross on the underside or an out-of-square edge
  • Machine stops mid-program with an axis or servo fault
  • Positioning repeatability lost, parts out of tolerance across the sheet
  • Assist gas pressure or purity problems that stop consistent cutting
  • Extraction or filtration no longer keeping the cabin clear
  • Control PC failing, HMI dead, or the CNC no longer boots
  • Machine runs but the laser will not fire on command

Control platforms and the software problem

On imported machines the control platform is often the part that decides whether a repair is straightforward or an expedition. Cypcut and FSCUT from Friendess, Beckhoff and Siemens based controls, Fanuc on older machines, and a long list of servo and drive brands including Delta, Yaskawa, Panasonic, Estun, Inovance and Leadshine. The hardware is usually obtainable. What is often not obtainable is the licence, the dongle, the parameter set or the machine-specific configuration, and that is a different kind of problem from a failed board.

Our scope covers the control and drive hardware, the I/O and safety circuits, and re-establishing a working configuration where the records have been lost. Where the right answer is replacing the control chain rather than nursing it, that is a retrofit, and we will tell you which of the two your machine needs rather than quoting whichever is larger.

Assist gas, extraction and the services nobody budgets for

Cut-quality calls can resolve in the services around the machine rather than in the laser or the head, and these are the items least likely to be on a maintenance list. Assist gas pressure, purity and regulation; a nitrogen generator or bulk supply that has drifted; compressed air quality; extraction and filtration that no longer clears the cabin; chillers running warm or with poor water quality; cabinet cooling in a hot summer shop. These are unglamorous and they are in scope, because a machine that cannot be supplied properly cannot cut properly no matter what is repaired upstream.

Consumable and filter life, like protective window life, is information. A change in how fast the shop is consuming something is worth reporting with the fault.

Machines whose builder is gone

Imported machines change dealers, dealers stop trading, and builders dissolve. What that removes is the documentation route, the parts route and the phone number, not the machine. A frame with good mechanics, a serviceable source and a dead control platform is a candidate for repair or for a control retrofit, and both are compared against capital replacement in writing before you commit to any of them.

What we need to start is modest: the machine model and serial number, the source model if you have it, the control platform name on the operator panel, and what changed. You do not need to open anything to answer that, and you do not need to identify the failed part before contacting us.

How a job runs

  1. What you send. Machine make, model and serial number, the laser source model if you have it, the control platform on the operator panel, what the machine does now and what changed. Photographs of labels and of the fault display help.
  2. Written scope and a fixed evaluation fee. Quoted before anyone travels, with what the evaluation covers, the expected turnaround and what a negative result would mean.
  3. What the evaluation covers. The as-found condition, the actual configuration rather than the recorded one, the machine and the laser as separable systems, and the services around them, with instrumentation named in the proposal.
  4. Written findings. What was measured, what was found, which subsystem the fault sits in, what remains unknown, and the options with their cost and risks.
  5. Your approval. Nothing is repaired, substituted or modified until you approve the scope and the price in writing.
  6. Work, validation and report. Inside the approved scope, then validation that the agreed scope was met, and a written service report you keep.

Questions owners ask

My machine is down and production is stopped. What happens first?

Tell us the machine, the control platform, what it reports and what changed. We reply with an assessment and, where a site visit is the right shape, a written scope and a fixed evaluation fee before anyone travels. Call (732) 516-8880 if it is faster than typing.

The machine still cuts but the parts are out of tolerance. Is that a laser job?

Not necessarily. Positioning, repeatability, drives, the table and the frame are machine questions, and they produce parts that fail inspection while the laser is healthy. Separating the two is the first thing the evaluation does.

Can you support a machine whose builder no longer exists?

Yes. That is a documentation and parts problem rather than a machine problem. See machines whose builder is gone, and where the answer is replacing rather than nursing a subsystem, the retrofit page covers it.

Do you work on tube and combination machines as well as flat-bed?

Yes, along with coil-fed and blanking lines, plate gantries and robotic cells built on the same optics. Tell us which it is, because the motion and handling questions differ even when the laser does not.

Do you offer scheduled service as well as breakdown cover?

Yes. Monthly and quarterly preventive maintenance programs are set out on the preventive maintenance page, with the interval set per machine rather than by a calendar rule.
