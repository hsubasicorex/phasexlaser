When a delivery-cable inquiry belongs here

This page is for the high-power optical delivery path between an industrial fiber-laser source and its process head—not telecom patch cords or ordinary data fiber. Owners contact us when the machine is down, output has fallen, the armored cable has been cut or crushed, or heat or contamination is visible near the output connector.

Cut, crushed or burned cable

Mechanical damage to the armored delivery cable or damage close to the laser source or process head.

QBH, QD or equivalent output damage

A burned, contaminated or mechanically damaged output assembly that may be reported as a connector, process fiber or laser cable problem.

Low or unstable delivered power

A cable or connector suspected after the machine no longer delivers the expected process result. A symptom is not treated as a diagnosis.

Obsolete or difficult-to-source assembly

A delivery cable that the machine builder no longer supports, or a replacement whose geometry and connector must be identified before it is ordered.

Different names, the same service route

internal optical fiber assembly, pump fiber, signal fiber, combiner pigtail, source output fiber, machine builders, source manufacturers and maintenance teams use different names. Fiber laser cable repair, laser delivery cable repair, beam-delivery cable service, process-fiber replacement, laser output-fiber repair, QBH cable replacement and high-power fiber splicing can all describe this service route. The label and the physical configuration decide the scope—not the wording in the search box.

Output connector families are named as equipment identifiers — QBH, QD, HLC-8, LCA, LLK-D, LLK-B and equivalents. Naming a family identifies your hardware; it does not state that PhaseX holds a particular test system for it.

Inside the source: serviceable fiber assemblies

Not every fiber fault sits in the external delivery cable. An industrial fiber-laser source carries an optical path of its own, and it is a different service object with a different evaluation. Access, internal architecture and how the manufacturer built the unit decide what is separable. On several platforms these parts are one sealed assembly, and where that is the case the honest answer is replacement or routing to the manufacturer. Which it is, is settled after inspection and stated in writing - not promised beforehand.

Internal optical fiber assemblies

The fiber runs and terminations inside the source enclosure. Accepted for evaluation when the owner authorises opening the source and the unit can be made safe. Send the source nameplate, the output interface type and the fault history. Where an assembly is potted, spliced into a module or not offered as a separate part, replacement or manufacturer routing is the likely outcome.

Connector, end cap, cooling, armor and interlock assembly

A delivery cable is an assembly, not a single part: output connector and end cap, cladding or mode stripping provisions, water cooling, armor and strain relief, length, pigtail and the interlock circuit. Accepted for evaluation as an assembly. Send the connector family, cable length, cooling arrangement and interlock type together with the source identity. A fault in cooling, armor or the interlock is frequently reported as a fiber fault, and separating those is part of the evaluation.

Active and gain-fiber path — evaluation class only

The doped fiber that produces the light, and anything spliced directly to it. We accept this strictly as a source-level evaluation class: we establish whether the fault sits in that path and we report it. PhaseX does not offer repair of active or gain fiber and does not present an evaluation as a repair capability. The outcome is normally module replacement or routing to the manufacturer or a depot.

Pump-fiber and signal-fiber leads

The feeds between the pump diodes, the gain stage and the combiner. Accepted for evaluation as part of a source-level assessment. Send the source model, serial number, power class and the exact alarm text. Diode-side faults frequently resolve to module replacement rather than fiber work, and we say so in the written findings.

Combiner pigtails

The pigtailed inputs and output of the signal and pump combiner. Accepted for evaluation; what is serviceable depends on fiber type, termination style and whether the combiner is a separable item on that platform. Send the source model, the measured output behaviour and photographs of the accessible area. A combiner integral to a sealed module is routed, not opened.

Source-to-output fiber path

The run from the combiner to the output connector, including the end cap and the interface the external cable mates to. Accepted for evaluation with the source present. Send the output interface type - QBH, QD or equivalent - the source model and what changed first. This is the path most often mistaken for the external cable, and separating the two is the first thing an evaluation does.

Dedicated large-diameter fiber capability—commissioning status stated plainly

PhaseX is commissioning a dedicated workstation for fusion splicing and cleaving of large-core, large-cladding fiber at its Parsippany shop. Until that workstation is accepted and the specific cable class is validated, specialty splicing is not offered as a released service, and this page does not present it as one.

Inquiries and evaluations are open now. Whether an assembly can be accepted depends on the source architecture, the fiber and its termination, the connector, the extent of the damage and whether a defensible validation path exists for that class. A diameter or a power rating on its own does not establish suitability. Do not ship anything before PhaseX issues written intake instructions.

Current service status: inquiry and evaluation intake are open. In-house specialty splicing is released per cable class, and only after the workstation is accepted and that class is validated.

What to send before the laser moves

  1. Source identification. Laser-source make, model, serial number and a clear photograph of the nameplate.
  2. Cable identification. Cable label or QR code, connector type if known, overall length and photographs of both ends.
  3. As-found condition. Where the damage is, what the operator observed, when it changed and any alarm text—without disassembling the source.
  4. Machine context. Machine make and model, cutting or process head, and whether the source is still installed.

PhaseX reviews the evidence and replies with the next inspection step, what—if anything—to ship, the evaluation fee and the limits of the proposed work. Do not ship a complete source or an exposed fiber end without written packing instructions.

What an evaluation can conclude

Every class above ends in one of four written outcomes: repair within a defined scope; replacement of the assembly or module; routing to the manufacturer or a depot where the construction or the architecture requires it; or not economically repairable, stated plainly with the reasoning. Each accepted job carries a scope-specific validation plan and written release criteria agreed before work starts. We publish no universal power, beam-quality, back-reflection or thermal claim, and we do not release a job against criteria we have not agreed with you.

Scope, validation and safety

PhaseX does not promise that every damaged delivery cable can be repaired, does not infer suitability from kilowatt rating alone and does not claim original-manufacturer performance without the original acceptance data and an appropriate validation path. A written evaluation determines whether repair, replacement or return without repair is the responsible outcome.

High-power fiber-laser work is Class 4 laser work. Equipment is accepted only under a defined scope, with condition records and customer approval. PhaseX is an independent service company, not an authorized service centre for the manufacturers named here, and does not perform manufacturer warranty work unless expressly authorized in writing.

Questions owners ask

Can you splice a cut fiber laser delivery cable?

PhaseX accepts cut and damaged high-power delivery cables for evaluation. A repair is released only when the fiber, connector, source architecture, damage and validation path support a defensible written scope.

Do you repair QBH cables and process fibers?

QBH, QD and equivalent output assemblies, process fibers and armored beam-delivery cables are within the intake scope. The label and photographs are reviewed before anything is shipped.

Do you service IPG, Raycus, MAX, nLIGHT and JPT cable assemblies?

MAX Photonics and nLIGHT are backed by PhaseX service history. Raycus, IPG, JPT, GW and other sources are accepted for evaluation. Brand names identify equipment only and do not imply authorization or equal experience on every platform.

Is the in-house large-diameter splicing service available today?

Inquiries and evaluations are open now. The dedicated workstation has been ordered and is being delivered and commissioned. A specific splicing job is released only after the equipment and that cable class have passed the required acceptance checks.

Should I ship the whole laser source?

Not until PhaseX reviews the source and cable identification. The written intake response states what to ship and how to protect it.

Do you work on fiber inside the laser source, or only the external cable?

Both, as separate service objects. An external delivery cable can usually be assessed on its own. Internal assemblies - pump and signal leads, combiner pigtails, the run to the output interface - need the source itself, the owner's authorisation to open it and a safe state. What is serviceable is settled after inspection.

Is a power supply cable the same as a fiber cable?

No. A power-supply harness, DC bus wiring and interlock cabling are electrical; a delivery cable, pump lead, signal lead or combiner pigtail is optical. We keep the two apart in the quotation and in the report, and an electrical fault is handled as source repair rather than as fiber work.
